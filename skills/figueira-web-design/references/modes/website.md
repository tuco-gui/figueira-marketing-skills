# Mode — Institutional Website

## Job

Build a digital presence that explains who the organization is, what it offers, why it should be trusted, and what path to follow next.

## Information architecture

Start from the visitor's jobs, not from the internal org chart.

Consider:
- home;
- solutions/services;
- about;
- case studies/proof;
- content/resources;
- contact;
- audience- or service-specific pages when justified.

## Home

The homepage should quickly answer:
1. who the company is;
2. what problem it solves;
3. for whom;
4. how it's different;
5. what to do now.

It doesn't need to contain everything. It needs to route well.

## Editorial design

- use rhythm, hierarchy and composition to build authority;
- let content breathe;
- avoid a uniform "card catalog" feel;
- treat photography, illustration and data as brand language;
- preserve consistency across internal pages.

## Navigation

- labels the visitor understands;
- reduced depth;
- navigation CTA only when there's a genuinely high-value action;
- footer as a useful map, not a dumping ground.

## Content

Do not use lorem ipsum in the final deliverable.
Do not invent case studies, numbers, logos, or certifications.
Clearly distinguish validated content from hypothesis.

## SEO and accessibility

When in scope:
- semantic headings;
- titles/descriptions;
- coherent URLs;
- appropriate schema;
- crawlable content;
- contrast;
- keyboard navigation;
- useful alt text.
