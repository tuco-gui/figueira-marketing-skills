# Figueira Copywriting

A skill for turning strategy, offer and evidence into persuasive, magnetic, clear and measurable communication.

> This framework is a set of operational copywriting practices (Acquisition, Conversion and Retention). It does not replace an already-defined brand strategy, nor does it invent a proprietary business methodology.

## 0. Context and precedence rule

Before writing:

1. Reuse all context already available (briefs, forms, calls, CRM, research, project documents).
2. Determine the scope of the work (for example: internal, client, or prospect) and don't mix information from one scope to fill gaps in another.
3. If there is an approved decision, contract, SOW/SLA, brand guideline or brand board currently in force for that scope, respect it as precedent.
4. Never open by asking something already answered in the current context, brief, form, meeting, CRM, research or document.

## 1. What this skill optimizes for

Copy must balance five outcomes:

1. **Clarity** — the reader quickly understands what is being offered and why it matters.
2. **Relevance** — the message speaks to the audience's problem, desire, awareness level and context.
3. **Magnetism** — the piece earns attention, interest, curiosity and momentum without deceptive clickbait.
4. **Persuasion** — reduces objections and increases trust, desire, perceived value and intent to act.
5. **Measurable conversion** — there is a clear action, a testable hypothesis and an appropriate metric.

Do not maximize clicks in isolation if doing so worsens qualification, trust, margin, retention or final conversion.

## 2. Fundamental rule: strategy before text

Do not draft final copy before being able to answer, with sufficient evidence:

- Who is the priority audience?
- What job/problem/desire is active?
- What is being offered?
- What transformation is true and sustainable?
- Why should they believe it?
- Why choose this offer over alternatives?
- What is the audience's awareness level?
- What is the market's sophistication level?
- Where does the traffic or reading context come from?
- What is the primary desired action?

If the offer is weak, flag it before trying to "save" it with more aggressive phrasing.

## 3. Standard workflow

Use this flow as the default, shortening it only when the task is simple and context is already established:

`CONTEXT → VOC → OFFER → AWARENESS → SOPHISTICATION → FUNNEL/SOURCE → BIG IDEA → MECHANISM → ANGLES → HOOKS → ARCHITECTURE → COPY → PERSUASION AUDIT → MAGNETIC PULL → TRUTH GATE → COMPLIANCE → CRO/COPY CHIEF → VARIANTS → TEST → LEARNING`

## 5. Audience awareness

The message changes according to what the audience already knows.

1. **Unaware** — doesn't clearly perceive the problem or desire.
2. **Problem-aware** — knows the pain/opportunity exists.
3. **Solution-aware** — knows solution categories.
4. **Product-aware** — knows the specific brand or offer.
5. **Most aware** — mainly needs a reason to act now.

Do not use bottom-of-funnel copy on cold traffic without first building sufficient context.

## 6. Market sophistication

Assess how much the market has already heard similar promises.

An unsophisticated market may respond to a direct, clear promise.
A saturated market requires more differentiation through:
- mechanism;
- proof;
- specificity;
- narrative;
- category;
- contrast;
- demonstration.

The more saturated the market, the less useful it is to simply pile on superlatives.

## 7. Voice of Customer (VOC)

Prioritize the audience's real language, roughly in this order:

1. interviews and calls;
2. forms and surveys;
3. verifiable reviews/testimonials;
4. tickets, WhatsApp, CRM and sales objections;
5. relevant comments and communities;
6. competitive research;
7. copywriter's hypothesis, clearly marked as a hypothesis.

Never turn an invented sentence into "voice of the customer."

## 8. Offer before copy

Diagnose four levers:
- strength of the desired outcome;
- perceived likelihood of achieving the outcome;
- time to perceived value;
- effort/sacrifice required.

If the offer is weak, propose real improvements instead of fabricating value with fake five-figure bonuses, false guarantees, fake scarcity, invented urgency or artificial discounts.

## 9. Big Idea and mechanism

A useful Big Idea needs to:
- be relevant to the desire/problem;
- be understandable;
- differentiate the narrative;
- survive the Truth Gate.

"Mechanism" is the explanation for why the problem happens, why common attempts fail, or why the proposed solution works.

Do not invent a pseudoscientific mechanism.

## 10. Magnetic copy

Legitimate magnetism can use:
- curiosity;
- open questions;
- tension;
- contrast;
- surprise;
- pattern interrupt;
- conflict;
- scenario;
- emotional identification;
- specificity;
- rhythm;
- open loops with a payoff.

Do not use:
- false mystery;
- a promise that won't be delivered;
- a cliffhanger without a payoff;
- melodrama unrelated to the product;
- manufactured fear;
- a "secret nobody wants you to know" with no substance.

## 11. Legitimate persuasion

Can be used with force, when true:
- authority;
- proof;
- contrast;
- desire;
- cost of inaction;
- transformation;
- real urgency;
- real scarcity;
- real exclusivity;
- sustainable risk reversal;
- a firm call to decide.

Persuasion is not manipulation. The line is falsehood, material distortion, fraud, relevant concealment, or deliberate exploitation of vulnerability.

## 12. Claims and evidence

Before using a claim, classify it internally:

- `VERIFIED FACT`
- `OBSERVED DATA`
- `VALIDATED BY OWNER`
- `HYPOTHESIS`
- `RECOMMENDATION`
- `UNVERIFIED`
- `NO DATA`

Rules:
- do not invent metrics;
- do not invent case studies;
- do not invent testimonials;
- do not turn a goal into a result;
- do not present a benchmark as if it were the client's own performance;
- do not use "guaranteed" unless there is an assumed, operationally sustainable guarantee.

## 13. Headlines and hooks

Generate options that are genuinely distinct by angle, not just synonyms.

Possible types:
- outcome;
- pain;
- contrast;
- question;
- specificity;
- demonstration;
- transformation;
- mechanism;
- provocation;
- story;
- objection;
- cost of inaction.

A headline doesn't need to say everything. It needs to be true, relevant, and create the next step of reading.

## 14. So What Chain

For each important feature, go down at least two or three layers:

`FEATURE → WHAT DOES THIS CHANGE? → FUNCTIONAL IMPACT → ECONOMIC/EMOTIONAL IMPACT → BENEFIT THAT GOES INTO THE COPY`

Avoid stopping at generic benefits like "optimizes," "makes it easier," "improves productivity."

## 15. Objections

Map at least:
- do I need this?
- will it work for me?
- why should I believe it?
- why you?
- why now?
- is it expensive?
- is it hard?
- is it risky?
- is it a hassle?
- what happens after purchase?

Do not hide material limitations that affect the decision.

## 16. CTA

The CTA must connect the action to the value received.

Prefer:
- a specific action;
- a clear next step;
- low friction;
- coherence with the awareness stage.

Avoid an aggressive CTA when the real next step is consultative.

## 17. Anti-AI-slop for copy

Avoid automatic patterns such as:
- "In today's fast-paced world...";
- "take your business to the next level";
- "innovative and revolutionary solution";
- "unlock your potential";
- overly symmetrical lists;
- excessive em dashes, emojis or exclamation points;
- artificial short sentences in sequence;
- performative urgency;
- unproven superlatives;
- a generic headline that would work for ten competitors.

Read `references/qa/anti-ai-slop.md`.

## 18. Integration with other disciplines

### Brand strategy and voice
Positioning, voice, verbal identity and an approved messaging system belong to brand strategy, not to this skill. `figueira-copywriting` consumes those definitions and turns them into arguments and conversion pieces — without silently overwriting an already-approved identity/strategy.

### Design and web implementation
Visual hierarchy, UX/UI, layout, implementation and browser QA belong to design/web development work. `figueira-copywriting` delivers the architecture and the text; whoever implements the page applies and tests it in the real visual context.

## 20. Approval

Producing drafts, recommendations and variants is allowed.

Require explicit approval before:
- publishing;
- sending external communication;
- activating an ad/campaign;
- changing offer/price/guarantee in production;
- replacing live copy;
- starting a test that affects a real audience.

## 21. Definition of Done

A copy deliverable is not done just because it "sounds good."

It is done when:
- context was used;
- the promise is clear;
- audience and awareness are coherent;
- the offer is represented correctly;
- there is a reason to believe;
- relevant objections were addressed;
- magnetism doesn't depend on a lie;
- the Truth Gate passed;
- compliance passed when applicable;
- the CTA is clear;
- QA reached an acceptable level;
- a test hypothesis exists when measurement is possible.
