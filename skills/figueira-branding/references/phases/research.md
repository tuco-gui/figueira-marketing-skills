# Phase: Research and evidence (Gate 2)

## Objective
Extract actionable principles from category, competition and culture — without copying competitors and without inventing data. Research enters when it can materially improve the decision, not as a mandatory ritual for every `LEAN` project.

## What to research, in this order
1. **Category and relevant cultural trends** — what is changing in the context the brand competes in, not passing design fashion.
2. **Direct and indirect competitors** — who the audience actually compares this brand to, including competitors outside the obvious category if the audience perceives them as alternatives.
3. **Visual and verbal codes of the category** — what's expected/generic in the category (useful for knowing what to avoid or deliberately subvert).
4. **Saturated patterns** — what everyone is doing right now (avoid repeating without a reason).
5. **Underexplored territories** — strategic and visual white space within the category.
6. **Real language from customers/market** — when reviews, sales transcripts, surveys or any real material is available (never invent a customer quote).
7. **Reference brands outside the category** — repertoire to widen the range of possibilities, not to copy.

## How to use tools when available
- Web/documentation research for trends, competition and category codes.
- Browser for a visual survey of competitors (real screenshots, not descriptions from memory).
- Never state "market research shows X" without a real, citable source.

## Extract principles, don't copy
Every research finding should become a decision principle ("competitors use heavy serif typography to communicate tradition — white space: geometry + vivid color to communicate energy"), not a "let's make it look like brand Y."

## Evidence classification
Tag every finding with the vocabulary from `SKILL.md`: `VERIFIED FACT`, `OBSERVED DATA`, `VALIDATED BY OWNER`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `RECOMMENDATION`, `UNVERIFIED`, `NO DATA`. Market perception, awareness, NPS and social proof without a real source are always `NO DATA` or `HYPOTHESIS` — never `VERIFIED FACT`.

## When to skip deep research
In `LEAN`, a quick scan of direct competition and category codes is usually enough. Extensive research is justified when the decision carries high risk (`DEEP` rebrand) or when the category is new to the team.
