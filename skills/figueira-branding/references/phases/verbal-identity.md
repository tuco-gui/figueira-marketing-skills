# Phase: Verbal identity (Gate 5)

## Objective
Translate the brand personality defined in strategy into a consistent, applicable voice — without falling into caricature.

## Elements to define
1. **Voice traits** — 3-5 concrete voice traits, each with a "we do / we don't" pair to make it actionable (e.g., "Direct — we get straight to the point / we don't pad things out with unnecessary technical jargon").
2. **Tone by context** — the voice stays constant, but tone varies (e.g., lighter on social, more formal in a sales proposal, more empathetic in support). Name at least 2-3 contexts relevant to the business.
3. **Proprietary/allowed vocabulary** — terms the brand uses distinctively or consistently.
4. **Words and clichés to avoid** — generic category terms or "AI branding" jargon that dilute the voice (see `references/qa/anti-ai-slop.md`).
5. **Message structure** — how the brand typically opens a communication, builds an argument, closes a CTA.
6. **Tagline** — only when necessary; not mandatory for every project. It needs to be born from positioning, not from a loose brainstorm of pretty phrases.
7. **Real application examples** — at least 2-3 examples of real text (headline, email, post) in the defined voice, to make the guideline testable rather than merely descriptive.

## How to test if the voice is distinct
Take a piece of text written in the brand's voice and ask: could this text belong to a direct competitor without sounding strange? If yes, the voice isn't differentiated enough yet.

## Common mistakes
- A "friendly and trustworthy and innovative" voice — generic adjectives any brand could claim.
- Confusing a distinct voice with writing in an exaggerated/caricatured way (`SKILL.md` explicitly forbids this).
- A decorative tagline unrelated to the real positioning.

## Output of this gate
An applicable voice guide (traits + tone + vocabulary + examples) ready to guide real copy on the Gate 9 touchpoints.
