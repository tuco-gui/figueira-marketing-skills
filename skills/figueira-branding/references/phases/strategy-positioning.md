# Phase: Strategy and positioning (Gate 3)

## Objective
Stabilize the minimum strategic foundation needed before any visual-identity decision. Visual identity is a consequence of strategy — never the starting point.

## Elements to resolve, in this order
1. **Category/competitive context** — what game this brand is playing, from the audience's point of view (not always the same as the business's formal category).
2. **Priority audience** — who needs to be convinced first; don't confuse "everyone" with priority audience.
3. **Relevant problem/tension** — what real audience problem this brand solves or what tension it mediates.
4. **Value proposition** — what the brand delivers that solves the problem above, in concrete terms.
5. **Differentiation** — why this brand and not the closest alternative.
6. **Positioning** — the synthesis of everything above into a mental territory the brand can defensibly own.
7. **Personality/brand characteristics** — how the brand behaves and sounds, derived from positioning (not a generic archetype glued on top).
8. **Proof/real capacity that sustains the positioning** — what the company can actually deliver that validates the promise.
9. **Brand architecture** — when there are multiple offerings/sub-brands, how they relate to each other (monolithic, endorsed, house of brands).

## Recommended output format
A one-page positioning statement with a direct structure: "For [audience], [brand] is [category/territory] that [differentiation], because [proof]." Followed by personality in 3-5 concrete traits (with a behavior example for each trait, not just a loose adjective).

## What not to do
- Do not invent a grandiose purpose ("change the world") just to fill a template — purpose without real proof becomes the kind of empty claim `SKILL.md` prohibits.
- Do not jump straight to "personality = Jungian archetype" without connecting it to the real value proposition.
- Do not accept vague positioning ("we're the best option for everyone") — if it's not possible to name who the brand is NOT for, the positioning isn't ready yet.

## Output of this gate
Strategy stable enough to guide naming (if applicable), verbal identity and visual territories — it doesn't need to be perfect or final, but it needs to be specific enough to eliminate options, not to allow any direction.
