# Mode: rebrand

## When to use
Relevant strategic change to positioning, audience, name or identity. One or more strategic foundations of the brand are changing — it's not just the visual expression that has aged.

## What changes in the default workflow
- Gate 2 is mandatory with an equity audit (`references/phases/equity-audit.md`) **before** proposing any change. Without this, the rebrand risks destroying recognition that still has value.
- Gate 3 (strategy) needs to explicitly name what changes and what is preserved — never change everything "just because."
- Requires a transition plan: how the current audience, partners and market will perceive the change without losing trust. This feeds into Gate 12 (handoff) as rollout.
- The three visual territories (Gate 6) need to demonstrate how each one handles the identified equity — they are not "from scratch" territories.

## Critical distinction: rebrand vs. brand-refresh
Ask before classifying:
- Has the target audience changed? Has the value proposition changed? Is the name changing? → `rebrand`.
- Is the strategy still valid and only the expression (visual/verbal) is dated or inconsistent? →
  `brand-refresh` (see `references/modes/brand-refresh.md`), not this mode.

Misclassifying creates two opposite problems: treating a rebrand as a refresh underestimates the transition effort and the risk of confusing the audience; treating a refresh as a rebrand wastes equity that could have been preserved.

## Risks specific to this mode
- Confusing "leadership is tired of the brand" with "the strategy changed" — internal fatigue is not evidence that the brand needs to change for the market.
- Destroying name/logo recognition without measuring the real cost of doing so.
- Rolling out without explicit approval (never publicly change name/logo without sign-off).

## Minimum deliverable
Equity audit + brand problem + revised strategy + territories + system + transition plan.
Rebrand is never purely `LEAN` — even a small rebrand requires a minimum equity audit.
